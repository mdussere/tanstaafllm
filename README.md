# TANSTAAFLLM 🚀

> **There Ain't No Such Thing As A Free LLM.**

> A conspiracy to make a tiny, stubborn computer useful without pretending that any intelligence is free.

---

## The cause 🌕

Luna is a little handheld computer with the manners of a bootleg Game Boy and the ambitions of a free lunar colony. It is small, portable, imperfect, and thoroughly outgunned by the software industry’s appetite for RAM, watts, subscriptions, telemetry, and chrome.

We intend to give it an AI-native life anyway.

Not by cramming a datacentre into a plastic shell. Not by calling a distant API and declaring victory. Not by installing a beige desktop designed for a desk, a mouse, twelve windows, and a power socket that never needs to sleep.

**There ain't no such thing as a free LLM.**

Every token costs time. Every model costs memory. Every daemon costs battery. Every clever feature costs attention, maintenance, privacy, or a recovery path when it breaks at the worst possible moment. We are here to pay those prices consciously—or refuse the purchase.

## The crime ⚠️

Modern computing keeps asking modest machines to impersonate enormous ones. It treats a 1-GB handheld as an unfinished laptop, assumes the cloud is invisible, and calls frictionless the experience of handing over your data, your money, and your agency one tiny convenience at a time.

Luna has less patience for this arrangement. Its resources are brutally limited: a small battery, a tight thermal envelope, shared memory, modest ARM cores, a tiny screen, and buttons where a normal computer would have a keyboard and mouse.

So the system must earn every resident process, every dependency, every model, and every action it is allowed to take.

- No invisible compute.
- No fake certainty.
- No arbitrary shell disguised as conversation.
- No “just add another service” without naming its cost.
- No tyranny of the generic desktop.

We will build a machine that can say what it knows, what it can do, what it is spending, and when it needs help.

## The plan 🗺️

The revolution begins with embarrassingly practical work.

1. **Listen before we speak.** Make local voice input useful without silently importing a cloud or burning the whole memory budget.
2. **Make the world inspectable.** Give Mike structured state and finite actions instead of screenshots, guesswork, and coordinate roulette.
3. **Keep the brain humble.** Use rules, classifiers, or small models whenever they are enough; call frontier intelligence only when it is worth the bill.
4. **Develop in the open.** Let Mannie use normal developer authority, show its work, test it, and leave a route back to known-good ground.
5. **Win one constraint at a time.** Hardware facts before hardware fantasies. Measurements before mythology. Recovery before spectacle.

The point is not to make Luna look like a miniature workstation. The point is to make it become itself: a pocket-sized computer that understands its limits well enough to be surprising inside them.

## Our doctrine 📡

1. **The least sufficient intelligence.** A tiny deterministic action that works is more revolutionary than a grand model that merely performs confidence.
2. **State before pixels.** The machine should describe its own reality rather than force an agent to stare at screenshots and hallucinate controls.
3. **Boundaries are kindness.** Mycroft hears. Mike routes. Wyoh exposes. Mannie builds. Professor coordinates. None of them gets to quietly become all of them.
4. **Costs must be visible.** Memory, heat, power, latency, bandwidth, privacy, money, and attention are first-class design inputs.
5. **Recovery is freedom.** A system that can modify itself must also survive its mistakes.

This is not austerity for its own sake. It is the condition for making a machine that remains ours.

## A promise to end the tyranny 🕯️

We are not trying to overthrow an operating system with a dramatic speech and a Wi-Fi connection.

We are making a small, inspectable, self-improving environment in which intelligence serves a person rather than enclosing one. The tyranny is bloat, opacity, dependency, and the lazy assumption that more compute is the only kind of progress.

In the end, the big plan is simple:

```text
"Can it do this?"
"What will it cost?"
"What breaks if it is wrong?"
```

The answer should always be visible before the machine acts.
