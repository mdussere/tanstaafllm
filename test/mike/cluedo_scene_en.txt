Here is tonight's game, at Tudor Manor.

It is ten o'clock in the evening. Doctor Black has been found dead in the library, near the large window that looks out onto the park. Six people were in the manor that evening: Colonel Mustard, Mrs Peacock, Miss Scarlett, Professor Plum, Mrs White and Reverend Green. The manor has nine rooms: the hall, the lounge, the dining room, the kitchen, the ballroom, the conservatory, the billiard room, the library and the study. Six weapons were found in the house: the candlestick, the dagger, the rope, the spanner, the revolver and the lead pipe.

At seven o'clock, everyone had dinner together in the dining room. Mrs White had made a gratin, and Reverend Green said grace. During the meal, Doctor Black announced that he would change his will the next morning, and that one person at the table would be left out of it. He did not say who. Colonel Mustard laughed very loudly, Mrs Peacock spilled her glass, and Professor Plum left the table for a few minutes to fetch his glasses from the study.

At eight o'clock, the guests went their separate ways. Colonel Mustard and Reverend Green went to play billiards. They played three games without a break, and the maid heard them arguing about the score until after ten. Mrs White stayed in the kitchen to do the washing-up; the cook, who was tidying the pantry, confirms that she did not leave before a quarter past ten. Miss Scarlett said she was going to write letters in the lounge. Mrs Peacock settled in the conservatory with a book, and Professor Plum went to the ballroom to practise a piece on the piano.

At nine o'clock, the maid brought tea. She found Miss Scarlett in the lounge, writing, and she noticed that the cord of the lounge curtains had disappeared. In the conservatory, Mrs Peacock was asleep in her armchair, her book open on her knees. In the ballroom, the piano was silent and Professor Plum was not there; his jacket lay on the stool.

At half past nine, the gardener, who was closing the shutters, saw a figure cross the park from the ballroom to the large window of the library. The figure wore glasses that shone under the lantern, and held something long and supple, wound around one hand. The gardener did not see the face.

At a quarter to ten, Miss Scarlett heard a dull thud from the library, but she thought a book had fallen. She did not move, and the maid confirms that she was still in the lounge at five to ten, when she came back for the tray.

At ten o'clock, Colonel Mustard went to fetch a cigar from the library and found the body. Doctor Black had a thin mark around his neck. On the carpet, near the window that had been left ajar, there was a piece of curtain cord, traces of fresh earth from the park, and a crumpled sheet of piano music. The candlestick, the dagger, the spanner, the revolver and the lead pipe were all in their usual place, untouched, in the hall.

When he was found, at ten past ten, Professor Plum was back in the ballroom. His shoes were covered in earth, and he said he had only stepped out for some air.
